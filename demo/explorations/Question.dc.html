When do AI tutors improve students’ ability to reason independently?

Stewarded by the AIED question editors Synthesis v3.2 Updated Oct 30, 2026 38 records Compare 1 competing synthesis

Current best-supported answer

Well-supported, with conditions

AI tutors improve independent reasoning when they hold back answers and prompt students to explain their thinking first. When tutors give solutions on demand, short-term performance improves but delayed transfer does not, and in some studies it declines.

What changed in v3.2: two new studies with delayed-transfer outcomes showed that benefits depend on students generating an explanation before they get feedback. Earlier versions said only that “guided hints” help. See the diff

I teach 9th-grade algebra. Should I let students ask the tutor for worked solutions?
Probably not by default. The evidence suggests having students write their own explanation first, then giving hints. Solutions on demand raised quiz scores but not performance two weeks later. This holds best for middle and high school math. Evidence for 9th grade specifically is limited.
